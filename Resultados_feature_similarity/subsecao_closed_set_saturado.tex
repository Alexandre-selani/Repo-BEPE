\Cref{fig:plots_features} illustrates how features are distributed in the latent
space from agricultural benchmarks in contrast to the other class of benchmarks.
The space shown is that of the logits, where the MSP decision is actually taken; in
the two agricultural benchmarks it is only two-dimensional, so the panels are exact
rather than projections.

The two panels show the same structure. Each known class occupies a compact region with large logit magnitude, while the unknown class is
strung along the diagonal, concentrated in the low-margin region between the two
known responses: on Ceratocystis the network drives the two logits into a nearly
anti-correlated line, with Ground and Healthy at its two extremes and Ceratocystis
filling the segment in between and clustering around the origin.

This geometry is what makes a plain maximum softmax probability threshold so
effective on the agricultural benchmarks.~\cite{vaze2022} show that Open Set
performance is largely a by-product of Closed Set discriminative ability: across
architectures and datasets they report a strong correlation between Closed Set
accuracy and Open Set AUROC, and conclude that a baseline that simply thresholds the
maximum softmax is competitive with, and
frequently superior to, methods designed specifically for OSR. The
mechanism is visible in \Cref{fig:plots_features}: cross-entropy training pushes each
known class into its own high-magnitude, low-entropy region of the logit space, and
the better the Closed Set problem is solved, the further apart and the more confident
those regions become. Anything that does not belong to them lands near the boundary,
where the softmax response trends towards an uniform distribution and the threshold rejects it, with no
novelty-specific mechanisms involved.
